# In Loving Memory of Alan Stuart Linquist

*June 7, 1955 — February 26, 2025*

## The Heart Behind Translume

Translume is dedicated to the memory of **Alan Stuart Linquist**—a beloved husband, father, brother, uncle, and friend; a chemist by profession, an artist by spirit, and a man of extraordinary curiosity, knowledge, and care.

Alan lived with both rigor and wonder. He devoted 36 years to his work as a chemist, while outside the laboratory he found meaning in painting the landscapes around him. He was an accomplished plein-air artist, a student of film and music, a birdwatcher, a reader of history, and a careful keeper of family stories. He noticed details that others missed and remembered them with remarkable clarity. His life cannot be reduced to a diagnosis. He was creative, thoughtful, devoted, and deeply loved.

## His Struggle

In the final chapter of his life, Alan faced **dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma**, a rare and aggressive cancer marked by profound biological complexity and painful uncertainty. He and those who loved him were asked to navigate a disease for which answers were often incomplete, evidence was scattered, and the path forward was never simple.

Alan met that struggle with courage. Yet courage should never be used to soften the truth of what a patient and family endure. Rare cancer can take away time, certainty, comfort, and the future a family expected to share. It can leave loved ones carrying questions that medicine has not yet learned to answer. Translume was born in the presence of those questions.

## How Alan Helped Shape Translume

From its earliest conception, Translume was shaped by Alan’s experience and by the clinical data generated throughout his care. His records were never merely files, measurements, variants, images, or reports. They represented a person: his body, his suffering, his hope, his family, and the urgent need for medicine to understand more than any single document could reveal.

His journey helped define the principles at the foundation of the platform. Clinical evidence should not remain fragmented across reports and systems. Every important claim should be traceable to its source. Uncertainty should be shown honestly rather than hidden behind polished language. A hypothesis should never be presented as fact. Technology should support the judgment of clinicians and researchers without erasing the patient at the center of the work.

Alan’s experience made the purpose of Translume deeply personal: to bring scattered clinical and molecular information together, preserve its provenance, expose what is known and unknown, and help qualified experts ask better questions sooner. The goal is not to manufacture certainty. It is to make evidence more coherent, reviewable, and useful so that future patients and families are not left to navigate the same fragmentation and isolation.

## A Promise Carried Forward

Translume cannot undo what Alan endured, restore the time that was lost, or remove the grief carried by those who love him. What it can do is carry forward what his life and illness taught us.

Every time Translume connects a finding to its source, distinguishes evidence from speculation, makes uncertainty visible, or helps a clinician or researcher see a difficult case more clearly, it honors Alan. Every effort to understand a rare disease more completely, identify a testable mechanism, or spare another family from avoidable confusion is part of his legacy.

This platform is dedicated to Alan so that his experience may contribute to a future in which patients are understood more fully, rare cancers receive the attention they deserve, and families are met with clearer evidence, deeper compassion, and more meaningful options.

**For Alan—whose life was far greater than cancer, whose experience gave this work its purpose, and whose memory remains present in every effort to make medicine more humane, transparent, and worthy of the people it serves.**

### About Alan

Alan Stuart Linquist was born on June 7, 1955, and died peacefully at home on February 26, 2025, after a courageous battle with cancer. He was a devoted husband and father, a longtime chemist, an award-winning plein-air painter, a film and music enthusiast, a birdwatcher, a historian, and a genealogist.
