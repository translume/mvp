# Precision-oncology pathway report: dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma

## Case summary

Authoritative diagnosis: dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma. The analyzed specimen was an 80% tumor-content chest-wall soft-tissue tumor specimen, reported 06/05/2024. Principal findings are reported MTAP RNA underexpression with DNA copy-number loss; CDKN2A RNA underexpression; discordant/low-confidence CDKN2B findings; CHEK2 c.846+4_846+7del reported as loss of function; and a low-confidence, unspecified TP53 splice-region call. No matched normal was analyzed. Stage, disease extent, treatment setting, prior therapy, performance status, measurable disease, specimen timing, and trial geography are not reported in the supplied data.

## Overall interpretation

The most coherent molecular pattern is a possible 9p21 event involving MTAP and CDKN2-family cell-cycle regulation. It is relevant to the declared diagnosis because MTAP and CDKN2A/B alterations occur in a subset of dedifferentiated chondrosarcomas. However, the present results do not establish homozygous MTAP deletion, complete MTAP protein loss, p16/p15 protein loss, or retained RB function. MTAP-directed and CDK4/6-directed approaches therefore remain investigational and biomarker-contingent. The CHEK2 splice variant is biologically credible, but tumor-only testing cannot establish germline origin, biallelic inactivation, homologous-recombination deficiency, or therapeutic sensitivity. The unspecified TP53 call is not clinically interpretable without source documentation and validation.

## 1. MTAP-associated methionine-salvage vulnerability and 9p21 co-locus interaction

Reported MTAP copy-number loss plus RNA underexpression raises a potentially relevant investigational vulnerability in this dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma. Disease-specific genomic data support MTAP alteration as a genuine DDCS subgroup, but the result is not yet equivalent to the homozygous deletion and tumor-cell protein loss generally needed to assign an MTAP-null phenotype.

### Simplified pathway

```text
MTAP normally metabolizes methylthioadenosine as part of methionine and adenine salvage.
   ↓
Complete MTAP loss can allow methylthioadenosine to accumulate.
   ↓
Elevated methylthioadenosine can partially constrain PRMT5 and create greater dependence on residual PRMT5 activity.
   ↓
An adjacent 9p21 deletion may also involve CDKN2A or CDKN2B, connecting the metabolic event with altered cell-cycle control.
   ↓
This tumor has reported MTAP RNA underexpression and copy-number loss, but complete functional loss and the proposed metabolic state have not been demonstrated.
```

### Biological significance

A recent exact-diagnosis genomic series found MTAP alterations in approximately 19% of dedifferentiated chondrosarcomas, supporting disease relevance. Nevertheless, reduced RNA, heterozygous loss, homozygous deletion, absent protein, and elevated tumor methylthioadenosine are distinct biological states. Stromal MTAP can also modify bulk-tumor metabolite levels.

### Therapeutic strategies to investigate

- Investigational MTA-cooperative PRMT5 inhibition may be biologically relevant only if complete MTAP loss is confirmed. NCT05245500 studies MRTX1719/BMS-986504 in advanced solid tumors with homozygous MTAP deletion; the registry does not establish efficacy in dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma or this patient's eligibility (https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05245500).
- Trial assessment should use the protocol-specific MTAP biomarker definition rather than RNA underexpression or unspecified copy loss as a surrogate. This is a trial hypothesis, not a regimen recommendation.

### Potential combination or recombination pathways

- Combination hypothesis: if homozygous MTAP loss, p16/p15 loss, and retained RB dependence are independently confirmed, combined study of MTAP-associated metabolism and cell-cycle control could be mechanistically plausible. There is no dedifferentiated-chondrosarcoma clinical evidence establishing efficacy or safety for such a combination.
- Translational pairing—not treatment combination—of allele-specific copy number, MTAP immunohistochemistry, tumor methylthioadenosine, and PRMT5 pharmacodynamic measurements could distinguish a functional metabolic phenotype from a passenger or partial 9p21 loss.

### Potential bypass or resistance pathways

- Retention of a functional MTAP allele or MTAP-positive tumor subclones.
- MTAP-positive stromal or endothelial cells clearing extracellular methylthioadenosine.
- Failure of the tumor to accumulate methylthioadenosine despite a genomic deletion.
- Adaptive restoration or bypass of PRMT5-dependent RNA-processing and methylation functions.
- Cell-cycle progression driven independently of neighboring CDKN2A/CDKN2B status.

### Required confirmation

- Re-review purity- and ploidy-adjusted MTAP absolute copy number and determine homozygous versus heterozygous loss.
- Map deletion boundaries across MTAP, CDKN2A, and CDKN2B and assess focality and subclonality.
- Perform MTAP immunohistochemistry in malignant cells with internal MTAP-positive controls.
- Consider orthogonal deletion testing if the copy-number call is borderline.
- In a research or trial setting, consider validated LC-MS measurement of methylthioadenosine and PRMT5-output markers such as SDMA/H4R3me2s.
- Obtain stage, measurable-disease status, performance status, prior therapy, and geography before trial assessment.

## 2. CDKN2A/CDKN2B–RB cell-cycle checkpoint dysregulation

CDKN2A RNA underexpression and possible neighboring 9p21 loss are biologically credible in dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma. They do not, however, prove p16 loss or CDK4/6 dependence. The CDKN2B result is internally discordant, and RB1 status is unknown.

### Simplified pathway

```text
p16INK4A and p15INK4B normally restrain CDK4 and CDK6.
   ↓
Reduced inhibitor function can permit CDK4/6-mediated RB phosphorylation.
   ↓
Phosphorylated RB releases E2F transcription factors and permits progression from G1 to S phase.
   ↓
A 9p21 deletion can affect CDKN2A, CDKN2B, and MTAP together.
   ↓
CDK4/6-directed arrest generally requires RB to remain present and functional.
   ↓
RNA underexpression alone does not establish DNA deletion, protein loss, or an RB-dependent phenotype.
```

### Biological significance

CDKN2A/B loss is enriched in dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma and may contribute to cell-cycle progression. This patient's pattern is compatible with that biology, but CDKN2A is currently an RNA-level result, CDKN2B is discordant/low confidence, and no intact-RB inference is permissible.

### Therapeutic strategies to investigate

- NCT04040205 investigates abemaciclib in CDK-pathway-altered bone and soft-tissue sarcomas and includes a dedicated osteosarcoma/dedifferentiated-chondrosarcoma cohort. The registry requires a qualifying pathway alteration and retained RB protein, with additional disease- and prior-treatment criteria (https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04040205).
- CDK4/6 inhibition remains investigational in this setting; CDKN2A RNA underexpression alone does not support off-label use or establish expected benefit.

### Potential combination or recombination pathways

- Combination hypothesis: validated 9p21 loss and independently confirmed PI3K–AKT activation could represent parallel cell-cycle and survival pathways. AKT2 RNA overexpression alone is insufficient to establish pathway activation or a usable combination.
- Combination hypothesis: integrating an MTAP-associated metabolic strategy with CDK4/6 control would require complete MTAP loss, p16/p15 loss, intact RB, and dedicated safety and efficacy evidence. Such evidence is not available for dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma.
- Functional characterization should integrate DNA copy number, p16/p15 protein, RB1 status, phospho-RB, and proliferation markers rather than treating any single result as determinative.

### Potential bypass or resistance pathways

- Pre-existing or acquired RB1 loss or functional inactivation.
- Cyclin E–CDK2 activation that bypasses CDK4/6-mediated control.
- Cyclin or CDK activation independent of CDKN2A/CDKN2B.
- Parallel mitogenic or survival signaling, including PI3K–AKT signaling if independently validated.
- Intratumoral heterogeneity or subclonality of the 9p21 event.

### Required confirmation

- Review the original report page to resolve whether CDKN2B RNA was called overexpressed or underexpressed.
- Confirm CDKN2A and CDKN2B absolute copy number, zygosity, focality, and subclonality.
- Assess p16INK4A and p15INK4B protein in malignant cells.
- Determine RB1 sequence, copy number, and protein-retention status; do not infer intact RB.
- Consider baseline phospho-RB, E2F/cell-cycle transcription, cyclin E–CDK2 context, and Ki-67 as supporting or pharmacodynamic measurements.
- For NCT04040205 assessment, establish advanced/unresectable disease, prior anthracycline exposure or contraindication, performance status, laboratory eligibility, and geography.

## 3. Unconfirmed CHEK2–TP53 DNA-damage checkpoint impairment

CHEK2 c.846+4_846+7del has credible splice-disrupting evidence in the germline predisposition context. In this tumor-only result, its origin, allelic state, and functional contribution to dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma are unresolved. The accompanying TP53 call is low confidence and lacks an exact variant. Neither homologous-recombination deficiency nor drug sensitivity can be inferred.

### Simplified pathway

```text
DNA damage can activate checkpoint signaling that includes CHEK2.
   ↓
CHEK2 helps coordinate cell-cycle arrest, repair responses, and apoptosis through downstream effectors including p53.
   ↓
A functionally damaging CHEK2 splice event may weaken this checkpoint.
   ↓
A separately validated TP53 alteration could further reduce damage-induced arrest or apoptosis.
   ↓
The current data do not establish biallelic CHEK2 loss, functional TP53 loss, or homologous-recombination deficiency.
```

### Biological significance

The CHEK2 variant is more credible than a purely computational splice prediction because abnormal transcripts and loss of kinase function have been reported. Its role in this dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma remains uncertain. TP53 alterations are disease-relevant in DDCS, but prevalence cannot validate this patient's source-free, unspecified TP53 call.

### Therapeutic strategies to investigate

- No validated therapeutic strategy is supported by CHEK2 alone in dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma.
- PARP inhibition should not be inferred from this finding. A tumor-agnostic phase II olaparib study observed no responses among 14 CHEK2-altered tumors; this is negative pan-cancer evidence rather than DDCS-specific evidence (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10774963/).
- Any DNA-damage-response trial hypothesis would require confirmation of the alteration, tumor allelic state, independent repair phenotype, clinical setting, and protocol eligibility.

### Potential combination or recombination pathways

- Checkpoint-combination hypothesis: validated CHEK2 loss and independently validated TP53 dysfunction could jointly weaken arrest or apoptosis, but the direction of drug response cannot be predicted from this record and no regimen is recommended.
- Recombination biology: homologous-recombination function must be evaluated independently through validated core-gene, genomic-signature, or functional testing. CHEK2 status alone is not proof of homologous-recombination deficiency.
- Combination and recombination concepts must remain separate: possible dual-checkpoint impairment does not automatically establish a recombination defect or PARP sensitivity.

### Potential bypass or resistance pathways

- Retention of a functional CHEK2 allele.
- A splice event that retains sufficient functional CHEK2 transcript or protein.
- Compensation by other checkpoint kinases or p53-independent arrest pathways.
- Selection or restoration of alternative DNA-repair pathways.
- Absence of a true functional TP53 alteration despite the low-confidence report artifact.

### Required confirmation

- Orthogonally confirm CHEK2 c.846+4_846+7del and the applicable transcript/HGVS representation.
- Obtain blood or another non-neoplastic specimen to determine germline versus somatic origin, with genetic counseling when clinically appropriate.
- Review CHEK2 tumor variant allele fraction, local copy number, loss of heterozygosity, and possible second-hit status.
- If necessary, confirm abnormal CHEK2 splicing by a validated RNA assay and assess CHEK2 protein.
- Retrieve the exact TP53 genomic and transcript-level variant, read support, variant allele fraction, and orthogonal validation.
- Evaluate homologous-recombination function independently if clinically relevant, rather than assigning HRD from CHEK2.

- CHEK2 loss of heterozygosity or second hit: not reported in the supplied data.

## Cross-pathway interactions

- The MTAP, CDKN2A, and possible CDKN2B findings may reflect one broader 9p21 event, but codeletion boundaries and zygosity are unresolved.
- Complete MTAP loss could create a metabolic dependency while CDKN2A/B loss alters cell-cycle control; coexistence does not prove that either pathway is therapeutically dependent.
- Any metabolic–cell-cycle combination remains a research hypothesis requiring MTAP-null confirmation, p16/p15 loss, and retained RB function.
- AKT2 RNA overexpression might represent parallel survival signaling, but protein abundance, phosphorylation, genomic activation, and pathway dependence are not reported in the supplied data.
- Validated CHEK2 and TP53 dysfunction could affect checkpoint arrest or apoptosis, but this is distinct from homologous-recombination biology and does not establish HRD.
- Tumor heterogeneity could cause discordance among DNA copy number, RNA expression, protein expression, and metabolic measurements.

## Biological significance

The findings support a biologically coherent but incompletely defined 9p21 alteration affecting MTAP-associated metabolism and CDKN2–RB cell-cycle control in dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma. CHEK2 may impair checkpoint signaling, but its tumor role and relationship to the unvalidated TP53 call remain uncertain. None of these findings currently proves a functional therapeutic dependency.

## Clinical actionability

Clinical actionability is investigational. The strongest disease-matched avenue is the CDK-pathway trial context, contingent on a qualifying CDKN2A/B alteration, retained RB, advanced/unresectable disease, prior-treatment criteria, and other eligibility requirements. MTAP-directed trials require stronger evidence—typically homozygous deletion—than is presently available. CHEK2 alone does not support a DNA-repair-directed therapy.

## Required confirmation

Priority confirmation is: (1) allele-specific mapping of MTAP–CDKN2A–CDKN2B with MTAP, p16/p15, and RB immunohistochemistry; (2) resolution of the contradictory CDKN2B result; (3) matched-normal CHEK2 testing with tumor allelic assessment; (4) retrieval and validation of the exact TP53 variant; and (5) collection of stage, disease extent, measurable disease, prior therapy, performance status, and geography before trial review.
